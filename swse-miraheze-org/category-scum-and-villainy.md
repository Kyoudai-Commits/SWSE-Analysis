## [Fringe Heroes](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Fringe_Heroes "Fringe Heroes")

*Star Wars* explores the conflict between light and dark, but the *Star Wars* universe features numerous examples of antiheroes- individuals who fall somewhere in the middle and who could care less about notions of good and evil, instead forging their own ways, by their own rules. [Lando Calrissian](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Lando_Calrissian "Lando Calrissian"), [Han Solo](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Han_Solo "Han Solo"), [Boba Fett](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Boba_Fett "Boba Fett"), and many others take this role at some time in their careers, standing in that murky zone between hem and villain until circumstance or fate pushes them to one side or the other.

Such antiheroes are some of the most engaging figures in the *Star Wars* universe. Their inexorable slides to evil as well as their efforts to achieve redemption resonate with us all. Thus, characters of ambiguous morals and shady principals are often adopted in the roleplaying game. To this end, this chapter presents new [Character Creation](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Character_Creation "Character Creation") material to help you design rogue heroes that are every bit as compelling as any of the reluctant or fallen heroes found in the galaxy.

## Outlaw Tech

Whether you need to find someone, keep yourself hidden, cheat at [Games of Chance](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Games_of_Chance "Games of Chance"), or be ready with a surprise in a fight, you can make the difference with [Equipment](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Equipment "Equipment") not found in the bright stores of [The Core Worlds](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_Core_Worlds?action=edit&redlink=1 "The Core Worlds (page does not exist)"). Even if you prefer to keep yourself on the right side of the law, you might want to enhance that simple [Heavy Blaster Pistol](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Heavy_Blaster_Pistol "Heavy Blaster Pistol") with a few legal, if expensive, add-ons. This section is about making sure you have the right tools for your jobs, even if you have to ignore a few rules to get them.

Assassins, bounty hunters, and privateers have special needs, so they have special equipment to match. In addition to the [Equipment Upgrades](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Equipment_Upgrades "Equipment Upgrades") described in this chapter, your outlaw might find use for other unusual gear.

## We've Been Through a Lot Together

Sometimes, a [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") is more than just a means of transportation. Sometimes, it's part of a character's [Destiny](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Destiny "Destiny"). In fact, for some characters, it's the focal point of their [Destiny](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Destiny "Destiny"), which might otherwise be nothing more than to wander aimlessly from one jam to another. No matter how humble (or downright ramshackle) a [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") might appear, if it's the [Destiny](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Destiny "Destiny") of a character to be linked with that ship, it can accomplish amazing things.

If you decide to link a [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") (or, rarely, a [Planetary Vehicle](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Planetary_Vehicle "Planetary Vehicle")) to your [Destiny](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Destiny "Destiny"), that replaces the normal [Destiny](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Destiny "Destiny") mechanic. In essence, you don't have a [Destiny](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Destiny "Destiny") to accomplish any specific goal; instead, you're destined to have the most important events in your life with a specific [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship"). This option is appropriate in campaigns with at least a moderate frequency of [Starship Scale](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship_Scale "Starship Scale") encounters, but it's certainly not restricted to such campaigns. In fact, if a campaign centers exclusively around [Starship Scale](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship_Scale "Starship Scale") action, this option might be too powerful as it allows you to access your [Destiny Points](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Destiny_Points "Destiny Points") in most encounters. Only the GM can decide if Starship-linked [Destinies](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Destinies "Destinies") are appropriate for a given campaign.

You earn and spend [Destiny Points](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Destiny_Points "Destiny Points") with a Starship-linked [Destiny](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Destiny "Destiny") using the same basic rules as with the normal [Destiny](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Destiny "Destiny") options. You gain one [Destiny Point](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Destiny_Point "Destiny Point") at each level, and they never return once you spend them. However, having your [Destiny](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Destiny "Destiny") linked to a [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") causes the rules to work slightly differently.

First, if a [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") is your [Destiny](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Destiny "Destiny"), it is never completely destroyed. Even if it is blown up in battle or confiscated by [Hutts](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Hutts "Hutts") before being crushed and melted, some small part of it survives. Perhaps you find the hyperspace core the whole [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") was built around, or its ruined hull is found drifting as debris after that battle. Your [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") can cease to be functional as easily as anyone else's, but a recognizable part of it always survives for you to rebuild (or retrofit to a similar [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship")). It can be as time consuming and difficult as getting a new [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") would be (if not more so), but the result still qualifies as your [Destiny](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Destiny "Destiny")-linked Starship.

Secondly, the options you can exercise by using a [Destiny Point](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Destiny_Point "Destiny Point") are slightly different, as detailed below. The following are possible only when you are acting as a crew member ([Vehicle Pilot](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Vehicle_Pilot "Vehicle Pilot"), [Copilot](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Copilot "Copilot"), [Gunner](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Gunner "Gunner"), [System Operator](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/System_Operator "System Operator"), [Commander](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Commander "Commander"), or [Engineer](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Engineer "Engineer")) on the [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") linked to your [Destiny](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Destiny "Destiny"):

- Automatically cause an attack rolled against your [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") to miss, even once the attack is resolved. ([Commander](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Commander "Commander"), [Copilot](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Copilot "Copilot"), or [Vehicle Pilot](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Vehicle_Pilot "Vehicle Pilot") only)
- Automatically succeed on any [Pilot](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Pilot "Pilot") check, including any check to avoid a hazard or obstacle, getting the best possible outcome on the check. ([Vehicle Pilot](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Vehicle_Pilot "Vehicle Pilot") or [Copilot](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Copilot "Copilot") only)
- Allow your [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") to act out of turn, thus changing your position in the [Initiative Order](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Initiative_Order "Initiative Order"). ([Vehicle Pilot](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Vehicle_Pilot "Vehicle Pilot"), [Commander](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Commander "Commander"), or [System Operator](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/System_Operator "System Operator") only)
- Cause any damage rolled against your [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") to instead affect any other [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") within three squares. ([Vehicle Pilot](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Vehicle_Pilot "Vehicle Pilot"), [Commander](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Commander "Commander"), or [System Operator](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/System_Operator "System Operator") only)
- Move your [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") to the top of its [Condition Track](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Condition_Track "Condition Track"). If any systems have been disabled because of being moved down your [Condition Track](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Condition_Track "Condition Track"), they now function. ([Engineer](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Engineer "Engineer") only)
- Repair your [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship"), restoring a number of [Hit Points](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Hit_Points "Hit Points") equal to its [Damage Threshold](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Damage_Threshold "Damage Threshold"). ([Engineer](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Engineer "Engineer") only)
- Restore your Starship's shields to full [Shield Rating](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Shield_Rating "Shield Rating"). ([Engineer](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Engineer "Engineer") or [System Operator](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/System_Operator "System Operator") only)
- If you are using [Starship Maneuvers](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship_Maneuvers "Starship Maneuvers"), you can spend a [Destiny Point](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Destiny_Point "Destiny Point") to regain all uses of all your maneuvers. ([Vehicle Pilot](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Vehicle_Pilot "Vehicle Pilot") or [Gunner](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Gunner "Gunner") only)

In general, a character with a Starship-linked [Destiny](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Destiny "Destiny") can do what no one else can manage- unless they, too, are linked to its [Destiny](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Destiny "Destiny"). There's nothing to prevent multiple characters from being [Destiny](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Destiny "Destiny")-linked to the same [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship"). Such characters might share the ship, or might constantly be stealing, buying, or gambling it back and forth between them.

**Normal [Starships](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starships "Starships") and [Vehicles](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Vehicles "Vehicles"):** Unless a [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") or [Vehicle](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Vehicle "Vehicle") is linked to a character's [Destiny](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Destiny "Destiny"), a character cannot use a [Destiny Point](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Destiny_Point "Destiny Point") or any other ability (such as the [Jedi Master](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Jedi_Master "Jedi Master") class feature [Serenity](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Serenity "Serenity")) that automatically causes a specific outcome for an attack roll or skill check. [Destiny](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Destiny "Destiny") is intensely personal, relying on the individual's deepest connection to [The Force](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_Force "The Force")- something incompatible with these massive, lifeless machines. Other than having a We've Been Through a lot Together Destiny, only the *[Target Sense](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Target_Sense "Target Sense")* Starship Maneuver allows the use of [Destiny Points](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Destiny_Points "Destiny Points") and similar effects. Using this Force-based maneuver helps the character to let go of their conscious self (which is aware of and using the technology of the [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship")) and instead act on instinct, just as Luke did when he switched off his targeting computer during the Battle of [Yavin](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Yavin "Yavin").

### Hunks of Junk

Some [Vehicles](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Vehicles "Vehicles") aren't reliable. They are, in fact, notoriously unreliable. They require constant maintenance and might have systems failing in the middle of battle. A GM might decide a [Vehicle](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Vehicle "Vehicle") is Unreliable for any reason: he or she might require a [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") to be Unreliable as the price of making it part of a character's [Destiny](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Destiny "Destiny") (see We've Been Through a lot Together, above); a [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") might have been modified one too many times; or, it might not be receiving the maintenance it requires. Whatever the reason, once a [Vehicle](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Vehicle "Vehicle") is Unreliable, it causes problems.

Any time an Unreliable ship is moved a step down its [Condition Track](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Condition_Track "Condition Track"), you must roll 1d% on the below table, Unreliable Results, to determine the result. (If your [Vehicle](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Vehicle "Vehicle") ends up at least 3 steps down the [Condition Track](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Condition_Track "Condition Track") as a result of this movement, instead roll 1d20+80 to determine the result.) A character can spend a [Force Point](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Force_Point "Force Point") to reroll a bad result, but must accept the second result, even if it's worse.

A Glitch takes a [Full-Round Action](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Full-Round_Action "Full-Round Action") and a DC 15 [Mechanics](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Mechanics "Mechanics") check to repair. A Failure takes 1 minute and a DC 20 [Mechanics](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Mechanics "Mechanics") check to repair. A Disabled system takes 1 hour and a DC 25 [Mechanics](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Mechanics "Mechanics") check to repair.

## [Uglies](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Uglies "Uglies")

Uglies are Starfighters created by bolting parts of different [Starfighter](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starfighter "Starfighter") designs together. They are never built from scratch in their "Ugly" configurations, and they are rightly shunned by any legitimate naval power. However, given that [Starfighters](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starfighters "Starfighters") are built in modular parts and that much of the common technology in the *Star Wars* universe uses a centuries-old set of standardized sizes and connections, it becomes more practical than it would first appear to create such hybrid designs.

Uglies are generally found in the hands of pirates, slavers, smugglers, and other groups that have the ability to steal [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") parts (or buy them off the [Black Market](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Black_Market "Black Market")) but lack the resources to purchase or maintain conventional [Starfighters](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starfighters "Starfighters"). Many Uglies are created when a pirate fleet hijacks several large cargo ships and discovers they have the cockpits of one [Starfighter](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starfighter "Starfighter") type, and the engines of another. Others are built as one-off designs by techs trawling through the junk from [Sandcrawlers](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Sandcrawlers "Sandcrawlers") or space graveyards and old [Starship](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starship "Starship") battlefields. Most, however, are the result of having a mismatched group of second-hand fighters and none of the spare parts needed to repair them, so outlaw techs patch together working parts from whatever source they have on hand.

Because of their piecemeal nature, all Uglies are [Unreliable](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Unreliable "Unreliable"), even when receiving proper maintenance.

### [Equipment Upgrades](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Equipment_Upgrades "Equipment Upgrades")

No self-respecting bounty hunter uses off-the-rack components, and after a few years in the field neither do most commandos. Anyone with extensive experience, from smugglers to customs agents to adventurers, knows they might live or die by how well their gear works. Often, its not that you need a brand new piece of gear; you just need the [Equipment](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Equipment "Equipment") you have to work better. That's where Equipment Upgrades come in; they provide ways for you to get the most out of every kilogram you carry.

Even if you don't strictly need to upgrade your [Equipment](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Equipment "Equipment"), having a unique collection of tools and gadgets helps to build a reputation in a galaxy where it's best to get your foes to stand down without firing a shot.

## [Fringe Campaigns](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Fringe_Campaigns "Fringe Campaigns")

Crime, it can be argued, is one of the building blocks of society. Although illicit trade and commerce are certainly unethical, and perhaps even immoral, the rule of supply and demand sometimes prevents necessary goods from reaching those who need them most- except at exorbitantly high prices. Smuggling enables important cargos to reach their intended targets, without the costly and time-consuming red tape of a slow (and at times corrupt) bureaucracy. Certainly, the smuggler makes a profit from supplying needed goods; without profit, he or she could not afford to remain in business. Still, a reasonable markup is what draws the line between smuggling and profiteering.

Heroes in a Fringe Campaign might be those who have taken to a life of crime to make their fortunes, embracing expediency over ethics and using guile and force to do what others need hard work and cooperation to accomplish. Alternatively, the heroes could be noble-spirited individuals struggling to rise above their environment and make something of themselves, fighting the temptation to take the easy way out.

All this is rich, fertile soil for [Fringe Heroes](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Fringe_Heroes "Fringe Heroes") to grow and thrive- both as characters and as criminals who might one day come to control their own criminal empires.

## [Fringe Elements](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Fringe_Elements "Fringe Elements")

Although many crooks take to a life of crime looking for an easy way to make a credit or escape the consequences of a bad decision, in truth, life on the edge isn't easy. Once you move away from the safety of government- laws and its guardians- you're on your own if someone crosses you. Even worse, if you anger the wrong shadowy figure, you might find bounty hunters or assassins looking to even the score.

Even so, some prefer to live by their own wits even it means they have to keep their eyes open and a blaster handy. Some can't abide life under society's rules (be those fair or foul). Others already stand accused, rightly or wrongly, and prefer any free existence to life in the spice mines of [Kessel](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Kessel "Kessel"). Finally, a few can't keep away from the forbidden, preferring life in the underbelly of the galaxy exactly because others shun it.

## [Fringe Encounters](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Fringe_Encounters "Fringe Encounters")

This chapter is intended for Gamemasters and contains several mini-adventures ideally suited for outlaw heroes. If you're a player, you should probably skip over this chapter and wait for your GM to spring these adventures on you and the other players in your group.

Using Mini-AdventuresUsing Mini-Adventures

Occasionally, heroes take a tangent that their Gamemaster had not planned for, or the Gamemaster needs to fill a few hours' play with an entertaining diversion from the main plot. A Gamemaster can improvise something- but, when the Gamemaster is at a loss for a scenario (or is just pressed for time), a mini-adventure might just fit the bill.

The mini-adventures presented here cover a range of themes and Challenge Levels, from salvaging derelict [Starships](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Starships "Starships") to escaping a forced-labor camp. Each mini-adventure includes an outline of the events and challenges presented by the scenario, which the Gamemaster can use to construct a few "off-the-cuff" situations- or use as a jumping-off point for future encounters.

In addition, each mini-adventure also features a fully developed tactical encounter designed to serve as the climax of the scenario. The Gamemaster can use these as written, alter the details to play to the heroes' strengths, or completely redesign them to fit the needs of the campaign.The Gamernaster can also reference these encounters when designing their own mini-adventures and scenarios.

These mini-adventures are also designed to cater to different heroes abilities, as well as the players' interests. For example, combat-oriented heroes should appreciate [Boarding Party](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Boarding_Party "Boarding Party"), while stealthy heroes will like [Breaking and Entering](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Breaking_and_Entering "Breaking and Entering"). Heroes hoping to show off their piloting skills should enjoy [Wanted Alive](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Wanted_Alive "Wanted Alive"). Similarly, [Smugglers' Rendezvous](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Smugglers%27_Rendezvous "Smugglers' Rendezvous") is designed with smuggler heroes in mind, while [The Big Hit](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_Big_Hit "The Big Hit") is aimed at bounty hunter heroes. The group's fast-talking con artist should find that [Snow Job](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Snow_Job "Snow Job") makes good use of the [Persuasion](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Persuasion "Persuasion") Skill. That said, each mini-adventure presented in this chapter gives heroes of all classes a chance to shine, even if the scenario is not aimed at their competencies.

Each mini-adventure opens with a summery that outlines the plot and various encounters. This is followed by another spread detailing the climactic encounter of the mini-adventure.

Even though these mini-adventures are designed to be completely independent of one another, common threads run through all of them, allowing them to be strung together and run as a sort of "sub-campaign' within the main campaign. It is purely up to the Gamemaster's discretion how to use them.

## [The Fell Star](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_Fell_Star "The Fell Star")

The Fell Star is a complete adventure that can be used as a launching pad for a [Fringe Campaign](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Fringe_Campaign "Fringe Campaign"). Ideally, this adventure will involve a group of four starting heroes, and it focuses primarily on the seedy side of life in the *Star Wars* universe. Heroes can come from any background, though characters with connection to the fringe- especially [Scoundrels](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Scoundrels "Scoundrels")- should feel right at home. This adventure can be set during any [Era of Play](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Era_of_Play "Era of Play").

If you are a player, you should stop reading now so that you do not spoil any surprises for yourself. If you are the Gamemaster, please feel free to continue on. As the Gamemaster, you should also read [Point Nadir](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Point_Nadir "Point Nadir") before running this adventure.

The heroes, whether part of an established group of starstruck adventurers or individuals with no prior connections to one another, are contacted for potential employment by an Anjiliac Hutt named Prello. The action begins on the desert planet of [Tatooine](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Tatooine "Tatooine"), in the seedy underbelly of Mos Eisley spaceport.

[Prello the Hutt](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Prello_the_Hutt "Prello the Hutt") has lost one of her prized employees- a [Balosar](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Balosar "Balosar") thief named [Barin Trevina](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Barin_Trevina "Barin Trevina"). Besides being a skilled crook, Barin also possessed valuable information. Despite her best efforts, Prello cannot locate Barin. She does not know whether Barin has gone into hiding, been kidnapped, or killed. She is willing to pay good money to see him returned to her unharmed.

The heroes' investigations of Barin's disappearance takes them to several locations in Mos Eisley and eventually leads them off-world to the mysterious [Shadowport](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Shadowport "Shadowport") of [Point Nadir](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Point_Nadir "Point Nadir"). Can they find Barin before it is too late and recover the artifact known as [The Fell Star](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_Fell_Star_(Force_Artifact) "The Fell Star (Force Artifact)") from the bowels of [Point Nadir](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Point_Nadir "Point Nadir")?

**STAR WARS: THE FELL STAR**

***It's a time of adventure. Heroes and villains alike seek their fortunes across the galaxy. Nowhere is this more apparent than in Mos Eisley spaceport, a wretched hive of scum and villainy situated on the desert planet of [Tatooine](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Tatooine "Tatooine").***

***The [Hutts](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Hutts "Hutts") command a strong presence in Mos Eisley, but they are not the only criminals. Rival syndicates and cabals prowl the dusty streets, making profits at the expense of one another, as well as society at large. Inevitably these disparate groups collide, their conflicting interests leading to open strife.***

***As unrest grips Mos Eisley's underworld, [Prello the Hutt](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Prello_the_Hutt "Prello the Hutt") contacts a group of mysterious heroes, requesting their aid in locating one of her most valued retainers. Can they find him before his enemies do?***
