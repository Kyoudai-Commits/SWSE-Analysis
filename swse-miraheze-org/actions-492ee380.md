# Actions

> Source: [https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Actions](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Actions) · Retrieved 2026-10-09T08:07:35Z.

*Reference Book: [Core Rulebook](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Core_Rulebook "Core Rulebook")*

Every round, on your character's turn, you may take a [Standard Action](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Standard_Action "Standard Action"), a [Move Action](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Move_Action "Move Action"), and a [Swift Action](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Swift_Action "Swift Action") (in any order).

You may take a Move Action or a Swift Action in place of a Standard Action, but not the other way around. You may also take a Swift Action in place of a Move Action, but not the other way around. Finally, you may sacrifice all three of these Actions to perform a single [Full-Round Action](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Full-Round_Action "Full-Round Action") on your turn.

Full-Round ActionsStandard ActionsMove ActionsSwift Actions

[Full-Round Actions](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Full-Round_Actions "Full-Round Actions") consume all of your effort during a given round, effectively replacing all other Actions on your turn. Some uses of [Skills](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Skills "Skills") require a Full-Round Action to complete. Examples include bypassing a lock (using the [Mechanics](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Mechanics "Mechanics") skill), searching an area for clues (using the [Perception](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Perception "Perception") skill), and entering a [Force Trance](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Force_Trance "Force Trance") (using the [Use the Force](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Use_the_Force "Use the Force") skill).

A Full-Round Action can't span multiple rounds; for example, you cannot perform a Full-Round Action that replaces your [Move Action](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Move_Action "Move Action") and [Swift Action](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Swift_Action "Swift Action") in the first round and your [Standard Action](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Standard_Action "Standard Action") in the following round.

[Standard Actions](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Standard_Actions "Standard Actions") are usually the most important Action you'll take in a round, and it often consists of some sort of attack- swinging a [Lightsaber](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Lightsaber "Lightsaber"), firing a blaster, throwing a punch, hurling a [Grenade](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Grenade "Grenade"), and so on. You can perform one Standard Action on your turn.

[Move Actions](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Move_Actions "Move Actions") represent Actions pertaining to physical movement. The most common Move Action is moving up to your [Speed](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Speed "Speed"). Standing up from a [Prone](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Prone "Prone") position, opening a door, and drawing a weapon are also Move Actions. You can perform one Move Action on your turn, or two if you give up your [Standard Action](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Standard_Action "Standard Action"). With the exception of specific movement related [Skills](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Skills "Skills"), most Move Actions don't require [Skill Checks](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Skill_Checks "Skill Checks"). In some cases (such as shouldering a stuck door), [Ability Checks](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Ability_Checks "Ability Checks") might be required.

[Swift Actions](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Swift_Actions "Swift Actions") represent things that require very little time or effort can be accomplished. Some Actions, [Feats](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Feats "Feats"), and [Talents](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Talents "Talents") require one or more Swift Actions to perform. You normally get one Swift Action per round, but you can take a second Swift Action instead of a [Standard Action](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Standard_Action "Standard Action") or [Move Action](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Move_Action "Move Action"), and you can take three Swift Actions in a round if you give up both your [Standard Action](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Standard_Action "Standard Action") and [Move Action](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Move_Action "Move Action").

Multiple Swift Actions usually have to occur on the same round, or consecutive rounds, and some Actions require that the multiple Swift Actions be consecutive (that is, no other Action interrupts them). This is noted in the Action's description.

## Special Actions

Some Actions take such a negligible amount of time that they can be performed in addition to other Actions or they can happen out of turn.

### Free Actions

Free Actions consume almost no time or effort, and you may take one or more Free Actions even when it isn't your turn. Examples include calling out to your friends for help and taunting a foe. The GM puts reasonable limits on what counts as a Free Action. Reciting the epic history of the [Rodian](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Rodian "Rodian") hunter clans takes several minutes (or more) and therefore isn't a Free Action. You can't take Free Actions when you're [Flat-Footed](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Flat-Footed "Flat-Footed").

**Q:** I have a question about the Force Technique [Improved Sense Surroundings](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Improved_Sense_Surroundings "Improved Sense Surroundings"). Its description says that you can "use the [Sense Surroundings](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Sense_Surroundings "Sense Surroundings") application of the [Use the Force](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Use_the_Force "Use the Force") Skill as a Free Action rather than a [Swift Action](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Swift_Action "Swift Action")." Since there is no specific limit on how many Free Actions you can take (or when you can take them), it seems that you could make this [Use the Force](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Use_the_Force "Use the Force") check over and over again, as many times as you wanted. Because you could theoretically reroll until you rolled a Natural 20, this would let you automatically maximize your roll every time and recover all your spent [Force Powers](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Force_Powers "Force Powers") on top of that!

Would this trick actually work, and if so, shouldn't this be errata?

**A:** No, it wouldn't work.

First, there's the "unlimited Free Actions" part. While the rules don't explicitly spell out a specific number you can take in a round, they do make it clear that Free Actions aren't without limits: "The GM puts reasonable limits on what counts as a Free Action." It's not that Free Actions don't require any time or effort; rather, they require so little that it's generally not worth counting. If you do enough of them, they can add up to a significant amount of time such that it is no longer reasonable to continue calling them Free Actions.

For example, saying a single word or a short phrase (such as calling out to your friends for help) is a Free Action, but saying a long sentence or more (such as reciting the epic history of the [Rodian](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Rodian "Rodian") hunter clans) is not. Could someone then claim to perform 10,000 one-word-long Free Actions that, in succession, tell the epic history of the [Rodian](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Rodian "Rodian") hunter clans? Of course not- this sequence of "Free Actions" just simulates a bunch of Full-Round Actions, so the GM is allowed (and expected) to disallow it.

Second, all of the above is actually moot because [Sense Surroundings](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Sense_Surroundings "Sense Surroundings") only comes into play "when making [Perception](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Perception "Perception") checks to detect or observe targets." In other words, these two Actions are directly linked such that you make the [Use the Force](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Use_the_Force "Use the Force") check immediately before making the [Perception](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Perception "Perception") check. You don't get to make multiple [Use the Force](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Use_the_Force "Use the Force") checks, just as you wouldn't get to make multiple [Perception](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Perception "Perception") checks to detect a hidden sniper.

Clearly, [Perception](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Perception "Perception") checks aren't unlimited. Unless the GM calls for a [Perception](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Perception "Perception") check as a Reaction (such as when a new enemy comes into your [Line of Sight](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Line_of_Sight "Line of Sight") or to oppose a [Stealth](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Stealth "Stealth") check), you have to spend a higher-value action to initiate a [Perception](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Perception "Perception") check (such as a Standard Action to actively search for enemies not in your [Line of Sight](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Line_of_Sight "Line of Sight") or a Full-Round Action to search a square).

### Variant Rule: Free Actions Aren't Free

Each round, a character can perform a number of Free Actions equal to 1 plus their [Charisma](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Charisma "Charisma") modifier or [Intelligence](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Intelligence "Intelligence") modifier (whichever is more), with a minimum of one Free Action allowed each round. This encourages characters with a higher [Charisma](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Charisma "Charisma") or [Intelligence](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Intelligence "Intelligence")- the leaders and planners- to coordinate the group even when they don't have any [Talents](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Talents "Talents") that can come into play. Conversely, it encourages the heavy-hitters to "shut up and shoot" instead of trying to overplan their Actions, thereby keeping the tempo of the fight moving along.

### Reactions

A Reaction is an instantaneous response to someone else's Action, and you can use a Reaction even if it is not your turn. Examples of Reactions include making a [Perception](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Perception "Perception") check to notice a bounty hunter sneaking up behind you and instantly activating a [Force Power](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Force_Power "Force Power") to absorb damage from an incoming blaster bolt.

You may only take a single Reaction to any one Action or effect. A Reaction is always resolved after the Action that triggered it, though some Reactions (such as the *[Negate Energy](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Negate_Energy "Negate Energy")* Force Power, or the **[Block](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Block "Block")** and **[Deflect](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Deflect "Deflect")** Talents) can affect the outcomes of the Action that triggers a Reaction.

## Initiative Actions

Usually you act as soon as you can in combat, but sometimes you want to act later, at a better time, or in response to the Actions of someone else.

### Delay

By choosing to Delay, you take no action when your turn in the [Initiative Order](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Initiative_Order "Initiative Order") arrives. Instead, you act normally at whatever later point you decide to act. When you Delay, you voluntarily reduce your own [Initiative Count](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Initiative_Count "Initiative Count") for the rest of the encounter.

When your new, lower [Initiative Count](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Initiative_Count "Initiative Count") comes up later in the same round, you can act normally. You can specify this new [Initiative](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Initiative "Initiative") result or just wait until some time later in the round and act at that time, thus fixing your new [Initiative](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Initiative "Initiative") result at that point. Delaying is useful if you need to see what your friends or opponents are going to do before deciding what to do yourself. The price you pay is lost initiative. You never get back the time you spend waiting to see what's going to happen.

*Example:* Deel and Vor'en approach a locked hatch, behind which they expect to encounter a crime boss and his thugs. Vor'en's [Initiative](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Initiative "Initiative") result is 22, but he Delays. He wants to open fire on the crime boss with his [Heavy Blaster Rifle](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Heavy_Blaster_Rifle "Heavy Blaster Rifle"), so he Delays.

On [Initiative Count](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Initiative_Count "Initiative Count") 14, Deel uses his [Mechanics](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Mechanics "Mechanics") skill to unlock and open the door. Now Vor'en can move through the doorway and fire a shot at the crime boss, but his [Initiative](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Initiative "Initiative") is reduced to 13 (just after Deel's [Initiative](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Initiative "Initiative") of 14). For the rest of the battle, Vor'en acts on [Initiative Count](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Initiative_Count "Initiative Count") 13.

#### Multiple Characters Delaying

If multiple characters Delay their Actions, the one with the highest [Initiative](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Initiative "Initiative") check modifier has the advantage. If two or more Delaying characters both want to act on the same [Initiative Count](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Initiative_Count "Initiative Count"), the one with the highest [Initiative](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Initiative "Initiative") check modifier gets to go first.

### Ready

Readying lets you prepare to take an Action later, after your turn is over, but before your next turn has begun. You can Ready as a Standard Action. To do so, specify the Standard, Swift, or Move Action you will take and the circumstances under which you will take it. Then, any time before your next turn, you may take the Readied Action as a Reaction in response to those circumstances (assuming they occur).

## Attacking and Readying

**Q:** Can you make an [Attack](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Attack "Attack") (Standard Action) and then Ready a Move Action in the same turn? I can't find any rule that says you can't, but older versions of the game wouldn't allow this.

**A:** No, it's not allowed. Readying an Action is itself a Standard Action regardless of the type of Action readied (Standard Action, Move Action, or Swift Action). This is apparently missing from the text, so it will be added to the errata.

## Reacting to Readied Actions

**Q:** If you Ready an Action that, when executed, makes it impossible for another character to finish its Action, what happens? Is the Action lost, or does that character get to change Actions? For example, if I Ready a [Withdraw](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Withdraw "Withdraw") to be triggered when an enemy makes a [Melee Attack](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Melee_Attack "Melee Attack"), would that enemy lose its Action?

**A:** If something occurs that renders an Action impossible or moot, that Action is lost and can't be replaced by another Action. This occurs in any such circumstance, such as a Readied [Withdraw](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Withdraw "Withdraw") as you describe, an [Attack of Opportunity](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Attack_of_Opportunity "Attack of Opportunity") that renders an opponent unable to finish a move or attack, a Readied [Move](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Move "Move") to get behind [Total Cover](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Total_Cover "Total Cover") in response to a [Ranged Attack](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Ranged_Attack "Ranged Attack"), a [Talent](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Talent "Talent") or [Feat](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Feat "Feat") used as a [Reaction](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Reaction "Reaction") that nullifies an attack, and so forth.

**Q:** Isn't that unfair? In the case of a melee opponent, I could keep that opponent from ever attacking by simply using a Readied [Withdraw](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Withdraw "Withdraw") over and over again.

**A:** On its surface, sure, it might seem unfair or even a little wonky, but careful consideration of what the rules represent can put this into perspective.

First, consider that time isn't broken up into neat little rounds in real life (or on screen). Really, the game uses a turn-based system just to keep the flow of time manageable and orderly during a chaotic fight. Your character's Action, then, isn't occurring instantaneously once every six seconds- it's evolving continuously over the round even though it is only resolved on your turn. Given this, when a character takes an Action (for example, a [Melee Attack](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Melee_Attack "Melee Attack")), there has probably been some build-up to enable that action. The lead time might be as little as a fraction of a second (such as with some Swift Actions, for example), but others might take several seconds to unfold (such as with many Standard Actions and Full-Round Actions).

Logically, if something stops you from completing your Action, you wouldn't have time to go back and start a completely different Action. In the case of a [Melee Attack](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Melee_Attack "Melee Attack"), the attacker has probably been thrusting, parrying, and slashing at his target for at least a second or two as he tries to find (or create) an opening. This rather long action is resolved as a single attack roll, and if it's "spoiled," you don't have time to set up something else.

Even a more discrete attack- such as that at the end of a [Charge](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Charge "Charge")- has its own problems if the target backs up or sidesteps out of harm's way. Once you begin that final swing, stab, or thrust, you're throwing all your weight into the blow. You can't "unswing" and conserve all the momentum you've been building to make the attack somewhere else. (If you've ever played baseball, consider how hard it is to "check" a swing when you realize a pitch is no good. Now, imagine trying to check a swing and then swing again a split second later!)

Second, it might seem unfair that a melee character can never make an attack, but remember that a defender who repeatedly uses Readied [Withdraws](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Withdraws "Withdraws") *also* can't attack. Their Standard Action is already committed to the Readied Action. Therefore, there is no net gain for the Readied [Withdraw](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Withdraw "Withdraw"), or for any number of Actions that can "spoil" another character's Action.

Third, the end result is perfectly balanced in that it doesn't alter the outcome of the encounter in any way. After all, if you want to avoid a melee fighter, you usually can run away just as easily (using the [Run](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Run "Run") Action or two [Move](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Move "Move") Actions to keep your distance), so nothing is actually changing other than your [Initiative Count](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Initiative_Count "Initiative Count").

**Q:** Okay, but even with this explanation, it still seems wasteful and annoying to spend a round doing nothing because your opponent Readied an Action instead of running away. Is there any way around this?

**A:** Yes, there is. As described above, Actions aren't instantaneous- they evolve over the course of the round, and this applies to Readied Actions as well. For example, if you Ready an Action to [Attack](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Attack "Attack") a target with your [Blaster Pistol](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Blaster_Pistol "Blaster Pistol"), you'll be aiming at that target, finger on the trigger, from the moment you Ready the Action. Likewise, a Readied [Melee Attack](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Melee_Attack "Melee Attack") would have your weapon primed for a quick strike (again, think of a baseball player prepared for a pitch), a Readied Action to draw a blaster would have your fingers twitching right next to your holster (think of the classic showdown at the climax of a western), and a Readied [Withdraw](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Withdraw "Withdraw") would have you bouncing on the balls of your feet, prepared to leap out of harm's way.

However, the description of the Ready Action doesn't say anything about your Action being hidden or unknown to others around you. Thus, other characters can almost always tell that you've Readied an Action and even what Action you've Readied (though they won't know the condition that will trigger it, of course). There might be some exceptions for Actions that have no obvious visible component, such as certain [Talents](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Talents "Talents") or uses of [The Force](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/The_Force "The Force"), but these should be adjudicated by the Gamemaster on a case-by-case basis.

Given this, it's not very easy to "spoil" an Action in the way you describe. If you Ready a [Withdraw](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Withdraw "Withdraw") to avoid a melee fighter, the fighter can tell that you've Readied a [Withdraw](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Withdraw "Withdraw"), and they'll probably conclude that you're trying to lure them in only to scamper out of range. Therefore, rather than approach you, they'll probably choose a different Action. In fact, they might Ready a [Charge](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Charge "Charge") that triggers the next time you take any Action at all, turning the tables on you!

#### Consequences of Readying

The count on which you took your Readied Action becomes your new [Initiative Count](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Initiative_Count "Initiative Count"). If you come to your next Action and have not yet performed your Readied Action, you don't get to take the Readied Action (though you can Ready the same Action again). If you take your Readied Action in the next round, before your regular Action, your [Initiative Count](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Initiative_Count "Initiative Count") rises to that new point in the order of battle, and you do not get your regular Actions in that round.

*Example:* Kelko and his friend Sia-Lan have just encountered a trio of [Tusken Raiders](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Tusken_Raiders "Tusken Raiders") in the wilds of [Tatooine](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Tatooine "Tatooine"). On [Initiative Count](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Initiative_Count "Initiative Count") 14, Kelko specifies that he is going to fire his blaster at the first [Tusken Raider](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Tusken_Raider "Tusken Raider") that tries making an attack. On count 10, Sia-Lan moves next to Kelko and Readies an attack with her [Lightsaber](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Lightsaber "Lightsaber"), so that she can strike any foe that comes within 1 square of her position.

On count 7, the [Tusken Raiders](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Tusken_Raiders "Tusken Raiders") charge, brandishing their [Gaffi Sticks](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Gaffi_Sticks "Gaffi Sticks"). As soon as the lead [Tusken Raider](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Tusken_Raider "Tusken Raider") raises his weapon, Kelko fires his blaster, but misses. Next, Sia-Lan swings at the first [Tusken Raider](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Tusken_Raider "Tusken Raider") to reach her and drops him. Other [Tusken Raiders](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Tusken_Raiders "Tusken Raiders"), however, reach Sia-Lan and attack her. From this point on, both Kelko and Sia-Lan act on [Initiative Count](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Initiative_Count "Initiative Count") 7 (and before the [Tusken Raiders](https://swse.miraheze.org/wiki/Tusken_Raiders "Tusken Raiders")).
